## Recording rules

- Record local time (Europe/Zurich), source (`remote` or `xiao`), and the state shown on the remote before pressing a button.
- Keep every packet class: short Pronto packets, non-standard symbol counts, and the canonical `n_sym=99` frame.
- Preserve the raw Pronto line before decoding. A decoded six-byte frame is an interpretation, not a replacement for the raw capture.
- Mark the physical result separately: no response, beep, fan movement, LED change, or unknown.

The campaign also corrects the checksum model. All FAN frames validate with the
general formula
`reverseBits8(reverseBits8(B1) + reverseBits8(B2) + reverseBits8(B3) + 0xCB)`
modulo 256. The earlier ambient-based expression was a COOL-only algebraic
reduction and cannot validate other modes.

The initial FAN 6 miss was not a frame-generation error: the sniffer captured
the expected frame. The identical explicit resend was accepted, so no automatic
repeat was added without broader reliability evidence.

OFF must always use the original remote's fixed frame. An OFF frame assembled
from the current setpoint and fan level can pass checksum validation and produce
a beep without stopping the unit. The firmware now returns the fixed
`D0 AC A8 08 03 A4` frame whenever `power=false`.

The Home Assistant automation also filters climate attribute changes: only HVAC
mode, target temperature, or fan mode changes emit IR. A retained ambient-
temperature MQTT update was verified not to trigger the automation or a new
sniffer capture.

## Current conclusions

1. The canonical control frame is 48 bits / `n_sym=99` and is validated for COOL and OFF.
2. Mode or state transitions can emit additional packet classes (`000D`, `n_sym=82`, `n_sym=104`) before a canonical frame.
3. FAN levels AUTO and 1–6 are now mapped from complete canonical captures.
4. The general checksum includes B1; the previous checksum expression is valid only for COOL.
5. Commit `931b3eb` implements the seven FAN levels and fixed OFF frame in the
   repository; the matching files were deployed, built with the live ESPHome
   instance, flashed over OTA, and physically validated as recorded above.
   Periodic auxiliary packets from the original remote remain separate future
   protocol work.

## Piste A phase 9 completion — 17:34–17:39

The remaining live-appliance tests used the Home Assistant climate path, except
for explicit identical-frame resends through the ESPHome service. DRY at 21°C
was acknowledged with a beep and produced behavior distinct from cooling and
fan-only operation. HVAC AUTO at 21°C was acknowledged, and changing its
setpoint to 22°C through Home Assistant produced another acknowledged command.

Identical AUTO/22°C/FAN AUTO commands were then used to characterize practical
reception cadence. The appliance acknowledged 9 of 10 commands at two-second
spacing and 8 of 10 at three-second spacing. At five-second spacing, Florent
observed 10 of 10 beeps. Fast identical commands therefore appear subject to an
appliance acknowledgement or reception window; normal human interaction is
reliable, and no automatic frame repeat was added.

The final Home Assistant command restored COOL at 22°C with FAN AUTO. Home
Assistant reported the same state, and the appliance acknowledged it with a
beep and resumed visibly cold airflow. Combined with the earlier FAN AUTO/1–6
and fixed-OFF tests, this completes the external-IR Piste A live validation for
COOL, DRY, FAN, AUTO, temperature changes, OFF, and practical repeatability.
